## London plane / lawn
- Authority: International Dendrology Society, Trees and Shrubs Online. [Species + photographer context](https://www.treesandshrubsonline.org/articles/platanus/platanus-x-hispanica/).
- Actual inspected photograph: https://www.treesandshrubsonline.org/site/assets/files/32674/platanus-x-hispanica-augustine-henry-3.jpg — `/tmp/vegetation-refs/plane.jpg`.
- Visible: canopy made of uneven overlapping branch fans with many sky holes; lobes merge into a continuous large silhouette rather than separate round balls. Outer foliage resolves as fine irregular leaf edges. Large branch forks remain visible through the middle. Root flare anchors the broad trunk. Sunlit upper/outer leaves are yellow-green; inner canopy is materially darker and less saturated. Strong vertical depth, despite broadly green overall appearance.
- Ground visible in same image: grass reads as a low continuous fine texture with slow broad variation, small pale dry areas, leaf specks and large mottled shadows. No uniform bright-green carpet or equally spaced upright tuft pattern. Base beneath trunk is darker/thinner with exposed root/soil contact.

## Oak branching
- Authority / primary inventory: Danish Dendrological Association, [Lord Nelson oak record](https://dendron.dk/dtr/detaljer.asp?Individual_ID=863).
- Actual inspected photograph: https://www.dendron.dk/dtr/photos/Lord-Nelson_HEL_MG_3507.JPG — `/tmp/vegetation-refs/oak.jpg`.
- Winter image isolates structure: main trunk progressively forks into angled, tapering, asymmetric scaffold branches; secondary branches turn and subdivide at different lengths. Avoid identical radial spokes and constant-thickness branch tubes. This is a woodland-grown mature oak, so do not directly copy its tall narrow lower-trunk proportion for every open-grown street tree.

## Glasgow birch / groundcover
- Primary observer photo / identification record: [NatureSpot silver birch](https://www.naturespot.org/species/silver-birch).
- Actual inspected photograph: https://warehouse1.indicia.org.uk/upload/med-14/78/53/o_1b0vpamqf1prm1rdsiv11pev136c8.jpg — `/tmp/vegetation-refs/birch.jpg`.
- Visible: slender pale stems with dark interruptions, slight lean and irregular fork heights. Fine outer twigs form a loose, airy veil, with much sky showing through. Branch tips bend down; narrow upper scaffolds point up. Pale bark is not uniformly white. Groundcover along path forms irregular joined fern/shrub masses of varied height and green/russet tones; dirt/leaf litter separates plants from path.
- Authoritative botanical corroboration: [Woodland Trust silver birch](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/british-trees/a-z-of-british-trees/silver-birch/). Specifies open canopy, drooping branches, dark basal fissures; notes downy birch is more common in Scotland. Current image is largely leafless, so leafy summer crown treatment remains inferred from species description, not directly seen in this photo.

## Glasgow conifer
- Authority: IDS, [Scots pine](https://www.treesandshrubsonline.org/articles/pinus/pinus-sylvestris/).
- Actual inspected photograph: https://www.treesandshrubsonline.org/site/assets/files/6733/pinus-sylvestris-1.jpg — `/tmp/vegetation-refs/pine.jpg`.
- Visible: tall bare tapering lower trunk, orange upper branching, uneven lateral needle pads with large air gaps; ragged flat-ish high crown. Needle clusters occur toward branch ends. A mature Scots pine is not a stack of solid Christmas-tree cones. Ground shrubs visibly overlap in asymmetric clusters, with shaded undersides.
- Species corroboration: [Woodland Trust Scots pine](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/british-trees/a-z-of-british-trees/scots-pine/).

## São Paulo ipê
- Primary local expert: botanist Ricardo Cardim, [Ipês-amarelos de São Paulo começaram a florir](https://arvoresdesaopaulo.wordpress.com/2008/09/03/ipes-amarelos-de-sao-paulo-comecaram-a-florir/).
- Actual inspected photograph: https://arvoresdesaopaulo.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/09/ipe-amarelo.jpg?w=640 — `/tmp/vegetation-refs/ipe.jpg`.
- Visible: yellow flowers group at uneven twig tips, with large transparent gaps and exposed irregular branching. Tall branching silhouette; flowers do not make an opaque yellow sphere. Leaves are sparse in this flowering photo. It is low-resolution, adequate for silhouette/cluster placement, not petal detail. Do not blanket the city with year-round yellow foliage using this seasonal state.

## Practical rendering priorities (inferences from the inspected images)
1. Fix silhouette hierarchy first: tapering rooted trunk → visible scaffold branches → asymmetric connected foliage fans → fine porous leaf perimeter.
2. Carry lighting through canopy depth: dark cooler interior, lit warm-green tops, restrained leaf transmission only where light passes through thin outer foliage. Random per-leaf RGB noise cannot replace structure or lighting.
3. Distinguish species by branching, porosity, crown height and foliage scale, rather than only changing green hue.
4. Give grass broad low-contrast color/height patches plus fine surface detail; reserve larger tufts for edges and uncut ground. Shrubs need connected leafy volume with edge breakup and contact shade, not separated identical balls.
5. Preserve paths and lot footprints. Stronger shadows/contact and small soil/litter transitions anchor all planting without filling circulation space.
