# Facial likeness

Match the camera before changing the face. Perspective, focal length, head tilt, expression and lighting can mimic proportion errors. Use matched reference/render crops, equal head height, a neutral expression and a neutral-light turntable. A single perspective photo cannot determine exact depth.

Compare landmark relationships: eye spacing and tilt, brow ridge, nose bridge/width/tip, philtrum, lip contour, jaw angle, chin projection and ear placement. Establish skull and jaw masses before eyelids, pores or texture. Keep characteristic asymmetry; avoid turning the subject into an average symmetrical face. Distinguish geometry from beard, makeup, occlusion and baked shadows.

Use overlays for observable landmarks and side-by-side front/three-quarter/profile renders for volume. Document which differences remain uncertain. No generic face-recognition score or invented percentage proves likeness. A structurally valid GLB, higher polygon count or attractive render is not likeness approval.

Gate: preserve comparable renders and an explicit visual decision from the task owner or the task's agreed review criterion. If likeness is rejected, return to camera/proportions before adding rigging, garment variants or skin microdetail. Never claim identity from absent reference evidence.
