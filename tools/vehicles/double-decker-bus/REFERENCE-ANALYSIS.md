# London bus — reference analysis (before geometry correction)

Source: user-supplied New Routemaster model sheet (hero, right, left, four 3/4 views, rear). AI-generated, so left/right are nearly mirror images; we follow the sheet, not the real NRM's asymmetric staircases.

A. **Staircase** — a single glazed band at the FRONT third of the bus, on both sides in the sheet. It rises **towards the front**: starts at the top of the lower-deck window line about 40–45 % of the length from the nose, and climbs forward to the upper-deck window line just behind the front windscreen. It is about one window-height wide (≈0.9 m measured perpendicular).
B. **Interrupted windows** — the last 1–2 lower-deck bays nearest the front are replaced by the stair glazing; the upper-deck ribbon is continuous to the front windscreen (the stair band meets it from below).
C. **Body vs glass** — glass: upper ribbon, lower ribbon, stair band, front and rear screens. Body (red): roof, between-decks band (≈0.6 m tall, its front end cut by the stair diagonal — the red band above the stair glass follows the same slope), lower skirt, front lower panel, rear lower panel. Black: front mask around the windscreens and destination display.
D. **Pillars** — thin, evenly spaced vertical pillars inside both ribbons, aligned deck to deck.
E. **Upper windows** — ≈7 bays from rear window to front screen.
F. **Lower windows** — ≈5 bays between the rear engine panel and the stair band.
G. **Left vs right** — same body and window rhythm on both sides; the passenger door sits at the front, ahead of the front axle (keep it on the kerb side, three.js −Z / left); the rear lower corner has an engine louvre panel on both sides; rear: wrap-around upper rear window, large lower louvre grille, plate.

Error in the rejected POC: windows/glass decals were generated for one side only, and the stair band was built descending towards the front from the upper-deck rear — the opposite slope and far too long, cutting across both decks.
